# Unevaluated retrospective — Threads into Motion

Written 2026-10-29 after implementation and real-renderer inspection. This is one implementation's evidence, not an adopted language proposal or fidelity certification; see [the index](../README.md#what-these-proposals-are). The id derived from the title is `threads-into-motion`. Implementation and hashes are pinned in [README.md](README.md); measurements and capture hashes are in [verification.json](verification.json).

## 1. State of the result

The 30-second plate implements the narrative using released MotionScript 0.5.0 / Scene IR 0.5.0 capabilities on wire `0.2`. It compiles and validates without diagnostics. Brief fidelity is **partial**: the graphic sequence is implemented, while physical cloth, sound and perspective are simplified.

The loose-end join is measured from **evaluated draw items**, after camera transforms: maximum error **0.70671 logical pixels** on the 40 samples from 20.0 through 23.9s at 10 Hz. This does not establish a bound between samples. The 27.8s centreline has exactly one segment intersection; the foreground pass is asserted visible and later in draw order. Other times' crossing counts are not exhaustively checked. Caption separation and bounds are checked on draw geometry at 10 Hz, including stroke padding, with the subject area constrained to y=70…640. Bars and shuttle are intentionally allowed to exit, and comb/foreground crossing fragments are excluded from the generic sweep; their exits or representative poses have separate checks. Cubic control bounds are conservative, but filled-shape antialias coverage is not measured.

There is **no audio output**. Sound directions remain in the brief. Texture is vector striation and slight geometric irregularity, not a fibre material. Physical thread identity is conveyed through connected geometry and coral paint, not conserved material coordinates. The released contract does not supply a cloth simulation, 3D camera, or audio facility; some additional visual detail could still be authored with more sampled geometry, within the same language. No unsupported feature is silently claimed.

## 2. Hardest and most error-prone aspects

Ranked by observed correctness risk and rework, not invented time allocations:

1. **Keeping the loose end on the withdrawing path.** The first full-size 20.5s still showed a visible gap: the loose-end anchor used horizontal progress while trim used arc length through an edge curve. Root cause: **my implementation choice**, with a language-level coupling inconvenience, not an established runtime defect. Workaround: use the existing schema `trimCommands` sampler in the generator, include dense keys near edge turns, and assert the actual evaluated attachment. Result: 0.70671 px maximum sampled error. This repair matters more than adding decorative fibres.
2. **Source expansion despite modest scene size.** An initial generated source was 526,829 bytes, above 512 KiB; later crossing passes also exceeded the budget. Root cause: **explicit sampled path data in the language plus my choice to repeat geometry per paint pass**. Workaround: two decimal coordinate precision, 49 ribbon samples per side, and four cubics for the loose end. The final source uses 151 nodes yet consumes 91.77% of the source budget. The ribbon declaration plus its commands track accounts for 29,552 bytes, and the loose-end equivalents for 48,331 bytes. This is byte accounting, not a token measurement. No budget was changed.
3. **Crossing topology and paint order.** The initial looping gesture did not actually self-intersect. Root cause: **my curve design**. Workaround: an open figure-eight-like gesture with one measured centreline crossing at 27.8s, plus three fixed-order near-pass nodes. The darker reverse interval is separate. Static z-order is adequate for this chosen gesture; arbitrary moving crossings would need more authoring or a different representation. Filled contour interpolation can create narrow intermediate poses, visible at 28.4s; this remains a stylistic limitation.
4. **Verification and preview lifecycle.** A process-per-still Chromium approach did not produce captures promptly in this environment, and reusing a CDP profile initially read a stale port. Root cause: **tooling and my lifecycle choices**; no browser defect is established. Workaround: one browser, a fresh profile per invocation, explicit renderer readiness, direct `renderAt(t)` evaluation, and CDP PNG capture. Percentage rounding also put some named boundaries microscopically late; percentages now round downward at eight decimal places. Runtime checks initially treated partially transparent underpaint as fully opaque; framing checks now separate opacity from bounds. These are implementation repairs, not new language requirements.
5. **Surface realism and camera direction.** The 16s and 27.8s stills reveal a graphic mesh and shaded strip, rather than fibres and a shallow-angle surface. Root cause: **the 2D contract and the chosen approximation**, with limited remaining source headroom. Workaround: shared morph displacement, offset underpaint, parallel strokes, and small camera rotation. The ripple's propagation, physical shed, dense fuzz, true twist and audio remain gaps; no measurement establishes that new syntax alone would resolve them.

## 3. Suggested improvements

All suggestions below are **hypotheses**, unless explicitly identified as an implemented workaround. References to other stories are recurrence evidence, not adoption.

### GVR fixes and tooling with no language change

- **Shared real-renderer capture and draw-item assertions.** Extract the preview lifecycle, fresh-profile handling, stroke-aware bounds, random seeks and attachment checks into a fixture helper. This would remove this builder's custom CDP/HTTP/bundle code and generic checking code. A more detailed version could add captures around every edge turn and quantify join error at a higher rate. This recurs in Blue, Clockwork, MotionScript→Motion, Crane, Small Ideas and Circle. It is not a pending language capability. The baseline duration-fixture and typecheck failures should also be repaired separately so these checks can become useful green gates; that removes no scene data and would make subsequent regressions easier to identify.
- **Early resource report, with byte attribution.** Check source bytes before compiling, and report generated geometry/track contribution. This would remove manual byte accounting and avoid compiling already oversized candidates. It would let a denser version allocate fibres and path keys deliberately. Budgets recur in Packet, Clockwork, Copperleaf, MotionScript→Motion and Crane. Profile before increasing limits; this story is not evidence for changing them. Compiler profiling also recurs in Crane, but the single 3.07s observation here does not diagnose its cause.
- **Reusable trim-end attachment sampling.** A generator helper that returns the evaluated endpoint with camera mapping would replace the row-text parsing and custom join scaffolding here. It would support tighter edge loops, more detailed shuttle orientation and a continuous released-length pool without introducing dependencies between runtime channels. This is an existing-language tooling experiment, related to path progress/point binding in Packet, Vectors, MotionScript→Motion and Circle. `transform.autoOrient` is unsupported in the registry; it would address orientation only, not attachment or conserved length.

### Experiments using the current language

- **Cubic strip boundaries and adaptive keys.** The four-cubic loose strand is already implemented; test the same idea on the 49-point ribbon contour and prune unchanged endpoint keys under fixed visual checks. It would remove much of the 29,552-byte ribbon contour block and permit more fibre strokes or ripple poses in the recovered budget. Smoother edges and lower byte count are hypotheses until the replacement is compared. Circle also raises morph topology. Shared command topology is already released as `geometry.pathMorph`, not a missing pending feature.
- **Explicit shed and travelling ripple poses.** Use current morph tracks for parity-dependent warps and a few wavefront positions; correct the generator's timing so acceleration ends at 13s. These replace the static overpass shorthand and single bulge, rather than requiring a cloth node. More detailed tension and packing could follow if the byte budget permits. Measure the geometry and framing costs first. The shared displacement recipe is related to the driver theme raised by Moon, Packet, Vectors, Clockwork, Counter, MotionScript→Motion, Blue and Circle.
- **Camera bounds and loop contour experiments.** Derive fit keys from the evaluated subjects, and try compatible cubic poses that preserve a broader opening through 28.4s. This would replace manually chosen zoom values and the narrow interpolated pose; a richer version could follow the raised tip without losing the tail. Camera-fit recurs in Moon, Packet, Clockwork, MotionScript→Motion, Small Ideas and Circle. Keep the camera 2D and describe that limitation. No camera-fit capability is claimed pending or implemented here.
- **Crossing pass shape and texture.** Replace the rounded foreground stroke fragment with a filled contour segment and test tapered fibre endpoints. This would remove the obvious rounded-ended crossing underpaint visible at 27.8s, and permit subtler front/back cues. Counter and Crane raise dynamic paint order; this fixed pass is an existing-language alternative for one crossing. It does not establish that animated zIndex is needed.

### Language proposals

- **Path attachment / sampled point channels.** Explore a constrained dependency that samples a path after morph and trim, with explicit coordinate space, evaluation order, ownership and cycle rejection. It would remove the loose-end attachment keys and duplicated shuttle position calculations; a more detailed version could keep the shuttle, fibre ends and released strand on a changing surface. This is the recurring point-binding/driver theme, not an existing pending capability. First compare the tooling helper above. Dependencies change sampling semantics and require a concrete design and conformance cases before adoption.
- **Animated width, then possibly a width profile along a path.** Uniform animated stroke width could remove duplicate weight passes in other stories, but would **not** express this film's travelling widening. A gated width profile with defined arc-length parameterisation, interpolation, joins, normals and cusp behaviour might replace this film's filled ribbon outline and reduce repeated contour coordinates. It could support finer travelling flattening and tapered fibres. Animated stroke width recurs in Small Ideas and Circle and is not pending in the inspected MotionScript pending file. A variable-width profile is a larger, untested extension; compare the current-language cubic experiment first. Pending `paint.animatedSolidFill` excludes stroke and does not address this problem.
- **Two-sided surface or depth ordering: defer specification.** Crane already raises two-sided panels and hinge transforms; Counter and Crane raise dynamic ordering. These might eventually replace the dark reverse-face and foreground-pass banks and allow a real twisting ribbon with changing crossings. This story alone does not determine a surface representation, lighting model or ordering semantics. It needs a design decision rather than a routine property addition. Masks/mattes would not by themselves supply a cloth surface.
- **Named beat times / seconds-valued key positions.** This would remove generator percentage conversion and its boundary-rounding hazard, and make denser action timings easier to edit. Packet, Vectors, Counter, MotionScript→Motion and Small Ideas raise it. It is not claimed as pending support here; equivalence with current percentage timing must be defined and tested.
- **Sound cues remain documentation for now.** All stories identify missing audio. Host-side cue metadata could eventually preserve rustle/tap/swish timing without pretending to render sound, but it removes no visual geometry here; a detailed soundtrack would require a separately specified clock and seek behaviour. No audio capability was added. General loops, functions, or a weaving execution model would require a separate design decision under AGENTS.md; repetition alone does not justify them.

## 4. Priority

**Fix now:** keep continuity and boundary checks, share the reliable Canvas capture tooling, add early source-budget diagnostics, and repair the unrelated base check failures in their own work. These offer correctness and usable feedback without changing semantics.

**Experiment next:** cubic ribbon outlines and byte attribution, then explicit shed/wavefront poses and a broader loop transition. Spend recovered source budget on the missing visible detail. Compare a generator attachment helper before proposing runtime point dependencies.

**Defer:** variable-width profiles, path dependency semantics, two-sided surfaces, audio, loops and functions until there is a scoped design and evidence from additional stories. Preserve the present caps and released contract. This implementation supplies a concrete test case and an honest list of gaps, not a language release recommendation.
