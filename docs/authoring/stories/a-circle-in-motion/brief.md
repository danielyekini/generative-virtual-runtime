# A Circle in Motion: brief

Direction script as supplied, recorded 2026-10-29.

---

Title: A Circle in Motion
Duration: 30 seconds
Style and tone: Fluid monochrome motion graphics. Black shapes and fine strokes on warm white, with generous negative space. Transformations preserve visible landmarks and continuous motion.
Setting and initial layout: A single falling drop above a thin horizontal pond surface. The central transformation stays near the middle of the frame.
Characters or subjects: One drop, its ripple, a clock, a bicycle wheel, and a rising sun. Their outlines and internal lines carry continuously from one form into the next.
[0:00–0:05] — Drop into ripple

* Camera: Fixed front view.
* Action and poses: A black teardrop falls from upper centre, stretching slightly as it accelerates. At 0:01.5, its tip touches the pond. The body compresses into a shallow oval, then spreads into a circular ripple seen obliquely. A small central rebound rises and settles. The ripple expands as its interior clears, leaving a closed outline.
* Timing: Accelerating fall, quick compression, then a gradually slowing expansion.
* Sound and effects: One soft water plink. No detached splash particles.
* Transition: The expanding ripple begins tilting toward the viewer.

[0:05–0:10] — Ripple into clock

* Camera: Hold the centre while gently pushing closer.
* Action and poses: The ripple’s ellipse opens into a face-on circle. The pond line retracts toward its intersections with the circle; those two remaining segments bend inward to become clock hands joined at the centre. Twelve short ticks grow inward from the rim. Settle the hands at approximately ten past ten.
* Timing: Complete the outline transformation by 0:07.5; resolve hands and ticks by 0:09. Hold for one second.
* Sound and effects: Water resonance becomes a quiet ticking rhythm.
* Transition: Accelerate the hands clockwise while keeping the clock rim continuous.

[0:10–0:15] — Clock into bicycle wheel

* Camera: Pull back slightly to leave room around the circle.
* Action and poses: The hands extend until their tips meet the rim, becoming the first two spokes. Each hour tick lengthens inward to meet the central hub, forming additional spokes. The outer circle thickens into a tyre, with a finer inner rim. Open strokes grow outward from the hub into a fork and partial bicycle frame; a second wheel outline closes at the left, establishing a side-view bicycle. Keep the transformed wheel on the right.
* Timing: Form the spokes during 0:10–0:12.5 and the bicycle context during 0:12.5–0:14. Hold the recognisable bicycle for one second.
* Sound and effects: Ticks quicken into a light freewheel sound.
* Transition: The bicycle begins travelling left to right.

[0:15–0:20] — Wheel into rising sun

* Camera: Track the right wheel, then settle as the bicycle’s surrounding lines withdraw.
* Action and poses: Rotate the wheel clockwise as the bicycle advances. Frame strokes retract toward the tracked hub; the second wheel opens at its upper edge and its contour withdraws along the frame. The tracked tyre remains intact. Its spokes shorten toward the hub while the hub expands into a black disc, absorbing them. A horizontal horizon draws across the lower frame. The disc dips partly behind it, then begins rising as the tyre outline merges into its edge.
* Timing: Preserve a clear rolling wheel through 0:16.5. Complete the sun silhouette by 0:19; begin its upward movement immediately.
* Sound and effects: Freewheel sound softens into a sustained low tone.
* Transition: Continue the sun’s ascent without a cut.

[0:20–0:25] — Sunrise held

* Camera: Fixed, with the sun centred horizontally.
* Action and poses: The sun rises fully clear of the horizon. Short rays extend from its edge, then curve subtly upward as the disc begins narrowing. The horizon remains still, grounding the image. Near 0:24, the upper edge of the sun draws into a rounded point, anticipating the drop.
* Timing: Rise steadily for three seconds; slow into a brief hold, then begin the deformation.
* Sound and effects: A soft tonal swell. Keep the silhouette crisp throughout.
* Transition: Retract the rays into the changing outline.

[0:25–0:30] — Sun into drop

* Camera: Ease back to the exact opening framing.
* Action and poses: Absorb every ray into the disc. Narrow the sun’s upper half into a teardrop point while its lower half remains rounded. The horizon shifts smoothly into the opening pond position. Rotate the teardrop half a turn so its point faces downward, then let it fall. Match the opening drop’s size, position, and downward speed at the loop boundary.
* Timing: Complete the drop shape by 0:28. Begin the accelerating fall during the final two seconds.
* Sound and effects: The sustained tone fades into the opening water ambience.
* Transition: Continue directly into 0:00, with the drop already falling.

Final visible composition: The original black drop descends toward the thin pond line, ready to strike again.
Contour continuity: Open paths grow or retract from visible endpoints. Closed outlines open only at an explicit moving gap and close when their endpoints meet. Preserve the main circle from ripple to clock to wheel to sun; use continuous deformation and visible line absorption throughout.
