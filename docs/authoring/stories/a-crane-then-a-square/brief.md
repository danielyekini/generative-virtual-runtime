# A Crane, Then a Square: brief

Direction script as supplied, recorded 2026-10-29.

---

Title: A Crane, Then a Square
Duration: 35 seconds
Style and tone: Textured paper cutout with soft contact shadows. Warm ivory front, muted blue reverse, and fine crease lines. Paper bends at folds while panels retain their shape.
Setting and initial layout: One square sheet lies ivory-side up on a neutral tabletop. A fixed upper-left light makes overlapping layers readable. The sequence uses a simplified crane fold with staged pauses; no paper sections appear, disappear, stretch, or pass through one another.
Characters or subjects: One continuous two-sided sheet. A tiny ochre mark near one corner helps track its orientation through folding and unfolding.

[0:00–0:05] — Establish the creases

* Camera: Overhead, framing the whole square with generous margins.
* Action and poses: Fold one corner to its opposite corner, exposing the blue reverse, then reopen. Repeat across the other diagonal. Turn the sheet over visibly, fold it in half horizontally, reopen, then repeat vertically. End with the blue side facing up and the crease network visible.
* Timing: Give each fold-and-open action approximately one second. Hold the completed creases briefly.
* Sound and effects: Light paper rustles and restrained crease sounds. Shadows tighten where surfaces touch.
* Transition: Keep the sheet’s orientation unchanged as the camera lowers slightly.

[0:05–0:09] — Collapse the square base

* Camera: Move to an elevated three-quarter view, retaining sight of all corners.
* Action and poses: Lift the side corners inward along the prepared creases. The upper and lower panels approach each other as the sides tuck between them. Flatten into a layered square base, ivory outside, with its open tips pointing toward the viewer. Let the near panel visibly cover the tucked layers.
* Timing: Collapse over three seconds; hold the flattened base for one second.
* Sound and effects: Soft layered rustle. Narrow shadows identify the separate open tips.
* Transition: Push in slightly toward the topmost layer.

[0:09–0:15] — Form the bird base

* Camera: Hold the three-quarter view. Rotate the entire model visibly when accessing its reverse.
* Action and poses: Fold the top layer’s lower side edges to the centre, crease the upper triangle, then reopen these preparation folds. Lift only the top open tip upward; its side panels hinge inward along those creases, forming a long diamond in a petal fold. Turn the model over and repeat on the other side. End with two long lower points and broader upper panels.
* Timing: Allocate three seconds to each side. Keep the petal-fold opening and inward collapse distinct.
* Sound and effects: Crisp crease sounds. Blue interior surfaces appear while each pocket opens, then become partly concealed.
* Transition: Hold the bird base with its narrow points directed downward.

[0:15–0:20] — Neck, tail, and head

* Camera: Turn slightly around the model to reveal the layered edges without reversing screen orientation.
* Action and poses: Narrow the two lower points by folding their outer edges inward on both faces. Open the layers around the left point and reverse-fold it upward into the neck; close the layers around its base. Repeat on the right for the tail. Reverse-fold the neck tip downward to form the head. Lower the two broad panels outward into wings.
* Timing: Narrow the points during the first two seconds; form neck, tail, and head over the next two; spread wings in the final second.
* Sound and effects: Small paper clicks at settled folds. Contact shadows show the neck and tail seated between body layers.
* Transition: Lift the completed crane just clear of the tabletop.

[0:20–0:24] — A brief flight

* Camera: Hold a close three-quarter profile, with the head left and tail right.
* Action and poses: The crane rises gently and performs two shallow wing flaps. Wings hinge along their existing root folds; the body flexes slightly in response. The nearer wing overlaps the body and briefly masks the far wing. Blue undersides become visible on each upstroke.
* Timing: Two measured flap cycles over three seconds, followed by a one-second settling motion.
* Sound and effects: Delicate paper flutter. The tabletop shadow broadens as the crane rises and tightens as it returns.
* Transition: Settle onto the table and return the wings to their pre-flight fold positions.

[0:24–0:29] — Undo the crane

* Camera: Ease back toward the earlier folding angle.
* Action and poses: Reverse the construction in order: raise the wings, open the head fold, lower the neck and tail by reopening their reverse folds, then release the narrowing folds. Restore the bird base. Turn the model visibly as needed to open each petal fold, lowering its long tip and returning its tucked side panels outward. Finish at the square base.
* Timing: Undo the crane features during 0:24–0:26.5; reverse the two petal folds during 0:26.5–0:29.
* Sound and effects: Soft unfolding rustles. Shadowed pockets brighten as layers separate.
* Transition: Keep the square base centred as its tucked sides begin to open.

[0:29–0:35] — Return to the sheet

* Camera: Rise smoothly to the original overhead framing.
* Action and poses: Separate the square base’s outer panels. Draw the tucked side corners outward along the original creases until the sheet opens fully, blue-side up. Turn the whole sheet over to restore the ivory face and the ochre mark to their opening positions. Let raised crease ridges settle against the tabletop.
* Timing: Open the base during 0:29–0:32; turn and settle during 0:32–0:34; hold for the final second.
* Sound and effects: One broad paper rustle, fading into silence. Retain faint crease lines and natural paper texture.
* Transition: End on the restored square.

Final visible composition: The original ivory square lies flat in its starting orientation, now carrying the crease history of the crane.
Layer continuity: Track both faces of every panel throughout. Moving flaps cast shadows onto the layers beneath them; tucked sections remain concealed until physically uncovered. Reverse the same fold geometry during unfolding rather than dissolving between shapes.
