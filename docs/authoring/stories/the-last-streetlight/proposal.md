# The Last Streetlight — unevaluated retrospective

This is evidence from one implementation, not an evaluated language proposal, adopted capability, or release. See the [story index](../README.md#what-these-proposals-are). Recorded 2026-10-29. Story id `the-last-streetlight` is derived from the supplied title. No registry, published version, `pending.md`, `CONTRACT.md`, or resource-limit changes were made.

## 1. Result

The complete 25-second picture compiles without diagnostics, validates with the default limits, and is available as the playground preset **The Last Streetlight**. The companion [movie](stills/the-last-streetlight.mp4) combines 750 production CanvasRenderer captures with an original, deterministic synthesized soundtrack. Native MotionScript playback has no audio channel; it remains silent. The builder produces the soundtrack and optional movie without requiring an engine change.

The picture uses black, charcoal and cold ivory, with the matchbook as the only red subject. The camera descends from the rooftops over 0–4s and settles into a magnified puddle view at 5s (frame 150 at 30fps). The elevated train crosses the window field in the opening, three diagonal rain layers continue across all five beats, and fine moving stipple supplies film grain. The shoe contacts the puddle at 5.5s/f165, five expanding elliptical rings break the reflection, and the second shoe stops by 6s/f180. The 6–8s tilt reveals the coat and fedora. The raised forearm and shielding hand enter before the 8.5s/f255 ivory match flare; the lower-face flash returns to darkness and the flame is out by 10s/f300. Smoke crosses the lens during 9.1–10.8s.

The briefcase figure crosses left to right during 10–12s/f300–360. The detective's head turns independently of the torso. Headlight geometry sweeps first, then a foreground car covers the alley during 12–13.5s/f360–405. The figure and car are both absent at 13.5s; a muted red matchbook remains at the gutter through the 15–17s push. Separate moving water contours divide around its cover. The advancing shadow stops at 17s/f510. The reaching hand withdraws at the first flicker; screen darkness reinforces the electrical failures at 17.5s/f525 and 18s/f540. Bundled vertical stems emerge from the shadow region, separate, and resolve into two lines of vector lettering during 18.5–21s/f555–630. A narrow diagonal beam sweeps left to right as the letters brighten. The entire title remains readable over 21–24.5s/f630–735; the small detective and steady light enter at 23s/f690. Broken, reversed letter contours shimmer below. A black plate fades from zero to full opacity over 24.5–25s/f735–750.

### Approximations and limits of verification

- The camera is a 2D crane, tilt and zoom, with a staged lateral repositioning and scale change for the over-shoulder composition. It does not create a perspective dolly or physically correct parallax. The detective remains a graphic silhouette rather than a fully articulated anatomical character. Head, forearm, feet and shielding hand provide the directed poses.
- Light uses translucent polygons, hard silhouettes, duplicate ivory face/flame geometry and timed black plates. There is no ray tracing, wet-material shader, volumetric haze or physically simulated shadow. Window haze is conveyed by low-contrast paint; water, splash rings, smoke and grain are deterministic vector choreography.
- Shadow-to-type is a match dissolve between a world-space shadow and screen-space elongated glyph stems, followed by position/scale tracks and contour fades. It is not a topologically continuous mesh transformation. At 18s the low stem bundle and flicker imply the connection; the legible contours begin at 18.5s.
- The title reflection is made from the actual glyph segments, reversed vertically, compressed, clipped into eleven horizontal bands in the generator, and offset by independent oscillators. It does not reflect arbitrary scene geometry or refract the rain. The puddle reflection is a separate stylized light shape.
- Sound cues are implemented as procedural noise and oscillators: rain, train rumble, splash, rustle, scratch, footsteps, tyres/engine, electrical buzz and clicks, bass, trumpet-like harmonics, piano-like chord and final drip. These are synthesized approximations of instruments and foley. The WAV and movie streams were mechanically checked; subjective listening quality was not independently evaluated. Sound is present in the movie, not the playground transport.
- Thirty-five exact-time stills were visually inspected via contact sheets, with full-resolution checks of the match flare, clue/flickers and final composition. Boundaries include 0, 5, 10, 15, 19 and 25s; the complete time list is in [measurements](stills/measurements.json). The 8.65s sample is a fractional frame (259.5 at 30fps), not a claim about an integer video frame. All 750 movie frames were rendered; visual inspection did not cover each one individually. This is native-story review against prose, not pixel fidelity certification against a reference movie.

## 2. Hardest and most error-prone aspects, ranked

1. **Coordinating framing, poses and screen/world transitions.** The opening train was initially above the visible frame at 2s; runtime screen checks caught it. The final crossing is staged earlier, with a 1s still. A path-based face flash initially inherited a check at its local origin instead of its drawn face point; correcting the assertion to the actual local face coordinates resolved the false failure. The clue push deliberately crops the detective's upper body, whereas the title must fit entirely. Root cause: manual 2D shot composition and our coordinate/assertion choices, with no fit/aim tooling. Workaround: camera keys, independent head/hand groups, real-viewport stills and explicit local geometry points in runtime checks. The still review also prompted the stronger pavement blackout and enlarged over-shoulder silhouette.
2. **Atmospheric density versus evaluated node cost.** A development build had 375 nodes and an observed 61.51ms evaluation mean. Replacing separate static building/window rectangles with equal-paint compound contours reduced the scene to roughly 245 nodes without removing windows; the final version has 246 nodes and denser rain. Intermediate means fell to 16.88–19.37ms. These were sequential development observations with different geometry and possible process contention, not a controlled speedup benchmark. Source inspection shows repeated node-index construction in graph traversal and per-item world/opacity resolution; its contribution is a hypothesis until CPU-profiled. Workaround: batch static equal-paint geometry, cache evaluated frames within assertions, and use compound tiled rain paths instead of hundreds of rain nodes. No runtime fix was made.
3. **Letter stems and a fractured reflection.** Eighteen glyph instances require separate fades, positions and stretch tracks, even though the visual direction is one title gesture. Reflection clipping required host-side segment intersection arithmetic. One empty clipped band rejected its entire containing group; its dependent tracks then produced cascading missing-target diagnostics. Root cause: absent text animators and masks, plus our generated-empty-path error. Workaround: explicit vector glyph networks, a harmless move-only path for empty bands, and generated clipping. The first still set used generic reflection dashes; review replaced these with actual reversed glyph contours.
4. **Synchronized audio outside the scene.** There is no released MotionScript audio channel. Root cause: representation/host scope, not a compiler defect. Workaround: seeded PCM synthesis with cues in seconds, then muxing with the deterministic 30fps render. This preserves every named sound cue in the delivered movie while keeping native limitations explicit. Native scrubbing cannot audition this soundtrack.
5. **Reproducible verification and syntax recovery.** The reference builder's SVG helper was unsuitable for visual review, so the more recent Chromium/CanvasRenderer harness was adapted. Early generator mistakes used six coordinates in one cubic tuple, a nonexistent `fill none`, and semicolons that separated stroke cap/join options. These were our syntax errors, not unsupported requested effects; compiler diagnostics led to corrections. Integer-time keys still need conversion to percentages, and exact documentary hashes need a separate commit pin. Workaround: one generator, optional real-renderer export, runtime assertions, measured source budgets and retained base-check logs.

They are not routine additions. |

The present implementation is the strongest tested workaround in this run, not proof that every alternative in current MotionScript was exhausted. For example, a topology-compatible path morph might improve part of the shadow transition, but matching a many-letter title to one shadow contour was not tested; this is a hypothesis, not a missing capability claim. Likewise existing radial paint and compositing effects could improve local haze or flame appearance; the current flat-geometry choice does not prove those effects impossible.

## 4. Priority

First, keep the still/cue/seek harness reproducible, fix primary-versus-dependent diagnostic presentation, and profile runtime graph work under fixed geometry before changing budgets. Address the existing typecheck and duration-fixture failures in a separate system change; this story does not silently change their expectations. A shared beat inspector and second-based cue labels offer modest, well-bounded implementation improvements.

Experiment next with masks plus reflected glyph strips, released text animator semantics when available, and a small host media clock. Compare output and costs against this exact program hash, including flicker boundaries, seeks, the 21–24.5s title hold and fade. Fixing playback cost should precede adding many independently animated raindrops.

Defer a general procedural language, particle simulation, perspective lighting and physically based wet surfaces until independent stories establish scope and conformance requirements. These could make a future version more impressive, but their performance and quality here remain untested. None is a reason to omit this story's current vector implementation.
